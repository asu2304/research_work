\begin{convbox}{Convention --- read this first}
PyTorch's \texttt{nn.Linear(in,\,out)} stores $W\in\R^{\text{out}\times\text{in}}$ and computes
$y=xW\T$. \textbf{Every matrix is shown as $W\T$ (shape $\R^{\text{in}\times\text{out}}$)} --- the
matrix $x$ multiplies from the left. Rows $=$ input dims, columns $=$ output dims.

\smallskip
\textbf{What this construction is.} It reaches the \emph{absolute implementable floor} for a
context-only predictor on the Markov-noise task --- the \textbf{ctx-count} distribution (the smoothed
empirical successor frequencies). Layers 1--2 are the inherited induction circuit: they \emph{count}
the in-context successors of the current state. The new pieces are two explicit, hand-set readout
operations (no trained weights):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item a \textbf{count recovery} --- read the per-state observation count $N$ from the layer-2
attention pattern via its inverse participation ratio $N=1/\sum_k A_k^2$;
\item a \textbf{count-domain (Dirichlet) readout} --- $p_i=\alpha\,(f_i N+\varepsilon)/(N+V_r\varepsilon)$.
\end{enumerate}
Unlike the \emph{equalize} head, it keeps the count \emph{magnitudes}, so it generalises verbatim to
an arbitrary Markov chain (unequal transition probabilities), where ctx-count \emph{is} the Bayes-optimal
context predictor.
\end{convbox}

\paragraph{Stream layout.} Same two-cell stream; the head reads $\fvec$ (the L2 output) and $A$ (the
L2 attention pattern), and emits a probability vector directly (no softmax temperature).
\[
\underbrace{x_t}_{\R^{\times220}}\!\xrightarrow{\text{L1}}\!
\underbrace{x^{(1)}_t}_{\R^{\times440}}\!\xrightarrow{\text{L2}}\!
\Big(\;\underbrace{\fvec_t}_{\text{successor freq}}\;,\;\underbrace{A_t}_{\text{attn pattern}}\;\Big)
\xrightarrow{\;\text{count-domain head}\;}\; p_t\in\Delta^{V-1}.
\]

% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Input Embedding}
Token $v_t$ at position $t$ is a concatenated one-hot $x_t=[\ev{20}{v_t};\ev{200}{t}]\in\R^{\times220}$.
The causal mask before each softmax is $M_{t_q,t_k}=-\infty\cdot\one[t_k>t_q]$. No learnable embedding.

% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Layer 1 --- The Strict Signal Retriever}
\emph{Inherited.} Suppress noise keys, forbid self-attention, and write the most recent ruled token
strictly before $t$ into dims $220{:}240$. Display shape $\R^{220\times220}$.
\begin{align}
(W^{(1)}_Q)\T &=\sqrt{220}\,I_{220}, &
(W^{(1)}_K)\T &= T_1\T=\begin{bmatrix}\zeros_{20\times10}&-10^9\one_{20\times10}&\zeros_{20\times200}\\
\zeros_{200\times10}&-10^9\one_{200\times10}&P\T_{200\times200}\end{bmatrix},\\
(W^{(1)}_V)\T &=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}I_{20}&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big], &
(W^{(1)}_O)\T &= I_{220}.
\end{align}
Recency sub-block $P_{t_k,t_q}=-10^9$ if $t_k=t_q$, else $-20(t_q-t_k)$. The score
$-10^9\one[v_k\!\ge\!10]-10^9\one[t_k\!=\!t_q]-20(t_q-t_k)\one[v_k\!<\!10]$ makes the most recent valid
ruled key a near-hard argmax.
\begin{invbox}{Layer-1 key invariant}
For every $t$, dims $220{:}240$ hold $\ev{20}{\vstar(t)}$ --- the identity of the most recent preceding
ruled token, $\vstar(t)=v_{t^*},\;t^*=\arg\max\{t_k<t:v_{t_k}<10\}$.
\end{invbox}

% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Layer 2 --- The Frequency-Averaging Induction Head}
\label{sec:l2}
Look up every position $t_k$ whose predecessor-ruled token equals the current token $v_t$ (= every past
occurrence of state $s=v_t$) and \emph{average} the tokens emitted there.
\begin{align}
(W^{(2)}_K)\T &=
\begin{bmatrix}\zeros_{10\times20}&\zeros_{10\times200}\\ \gamma\,\mathbf{n}_{10\times20}&\zeros_{10\times200}\\
\zeros_{200\times20}&\zeros_{200\times200}\\ I_{20}&\zeros_{20\times200}\\ \zeros_{200\times20}&\zeros_{200\times200}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{array}{l}\scriptstyle\leftarrow\text{ruled }0{:}10\\\scriptstyle\leftarrow\text{noise }10{:}20\\\scriptstyle\leftarrow\text{pos }20{:}220\\\scriptstyle\leftarrow\text{L1 out tok }220{:}240\\\scriptstyle\leftarrow\text{L1 out pos }240{:}440\end{array}
\\[3pt]
(W^{(2)}_V)\T &=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}I_{20}&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big],\qquad (W^{(2)}_O)\T=I_{220}.
\end{align}
$W_K$ reads the L1 predecessor identity and adds the noise filter $\gamma=-5\times10^4$ on noise
positions; $W_V$ copies the attended position's token; the query matches the current token against those
predecessor identities. With all matched keys tied, the softmax is \textbf{uniform over the $N$ matched
positions}, so its output is their value average:
\begin{equation}
[a^{(2)}_t]_{0:20}=\fvec_t,\qquad
[\fvec_t]_i=\frac{\#\{t_k:\vstar(t_k)=v_t,\ v_{t_k}=i\}}{\#\{t_k:\vstar(t_k)=v_t\}}
=\widehat P(\text{next}=i\mid s).
\end{equation}
\textbf{Crucially, this head also exposes its attention pattern $A_t\in\R^{T}$} (the per-query weights):
uniform $1/N$ on the $N$ matched positions, $\approx 0$ elsewhere. The next two operations read $\fvec_t$
and $A_t$.

% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Count Recovery --- $N$ from the Attention IPR}
\label{sec:count}
A frequency alone cannot be smoothed correctly: an estimate from $2$ observations deserves far more
shrinkage than one from $50$. So we must recover the per-state count $N$. Because $A_t$ is uniform over
the $N$ matched positions, its \textbf{inverse participation ratio} returns that count:
\begin{equation}
N_t \;=\; \frac{1}{\sum_{k} A_{t,k}^2}\;.\qquad
\Big(\text{uniform } A_k=\tfrac1N:\ \textstyle\sum_k A_k^2=N\cdot\tfrac1{N^2}=\tfrac1N\Rightarrow N_t=N.\Big)
\end{equation}
This is a hand-set, parameter-free read of an existing quantity (the attention weights) --- genuine and
architecture-internal.
\begin{invbox}{Count invariant}
$N_t$ equals the number of past positions that immediately followed an occurrence of the current state
--- i.e.\ how many times the model has \emph{seen} the transition it is about to predict.
\end{invbox}

% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Count-Domain (Dirichlet) Readout}
\label{sec:readout}
Renormalise the signal block of $\fvec$ to a distribution, $\tilde f_i=[\fvec_t]_i/\sum_{j<V_r}[\fvec_t]_j$
(so $\tilde f_i N=c_i$, the integer count of successor $i$), then apply \textbf{add-$\varepsilon$
smoothing in the count domain} and pin the exact noise floor:
\begin{equation}
p_t[i]=
\begin{cases}
\alpha\,\dfrac{\tilde f_i\,N_t+\varepsilon}{N_t+V_r\,\varepsilon} & i<V_r\ (\text{ruled})\\[10pt]
\dfrac{1-\alpha}{V-V_r} & i\ge V_r\ (\text{noise}).
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is an explicit probability (a divide-by-sum / ``$L_1$'' readout) --- \emph{no} softmax temperature.
It sums to $1$: $\alpha\sum_i\frac{\tilde f_iN+\varepsilon}{N+V_r\varepsilon}+(V-V_r)\frac{1-\alpha}{V-V_r}
=\alpha+(1-\alpha)=1$.

\begin{takebox}{Why this is exactly ctx-count --- and why it scales}
Substituting $\tilde f_i N_t=c_i$ gives
$p_t[i]=\alpha\,(c_i+\varepsilon)/(\sum_j c_j+V_r\varepsilon)$ on signal and $(1-\alpha)/(V-V_r)$ on
noise --- the \textbf{exact ctx-count formula} (Laplace/Dirichlet-smoothed empirical transition row).
Because the smoothing lives in the \emph{count} domain, the shrinkage is automatically stronger when
$N$ is small and vanishes when $N$ is large --- which is the Bayes posterior-predictive rule for a
Dirichlet prior. It keeps the unequal magnitudes $c_i$, so for a \emph{general} Markov chain (arbitrary
transition probabilities) it still returns the smoothed empirical row --- the Bayes-optimal context
predictor. The \emph{equalize} head, which flattens $c_i$ to a common value, only works for equiprobable
successors; this one does not assume that.
\end{takebox}

\section{Why the Earlier Readout Fell Short}
The previous count-readout used a \emph{fixed} floor on the \emph{frequency}
($p_i\propto \tilde f_i+\delta$), i.e.\ $N$-independent shrinkage. Decomposing the residual to ctx-count
(test split, last-$100$ window):

\begin{tabular}{@{}cccc@{}}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & attention-counting gap & smoothing-domain gap & verdict\\
 & (freq head $-$ exact-count head) & (exact-count freq head $-$ ctx-count) & \\
\midrule
0.1 & $-0.014$ & $+0.009$ & attention faithful;\\
0.5 & $-0.003$ & $+0.019$ & the whole gap is the\\

The soft attention counts faithfully (left column $\approx 0$); the entire gap was fixed-floor (frequency)

domain (\S\ref{sec:readout}) removes it.

For $\alpha\ge0.3$ the construction sits within $+0.004$ to $+0.007$ nats of ctx-count (it lands
\emph{on} the floor). With an exact $N$ (an attention-sink counter rather than the IPR) it matches or
beats ctx-count at every $\alpha$.

\begin{invbox}{The only residual --- and its fix}
At low $\alpha$ a state is observed $\sim0$--$1$ times, so the attention has no clean match and the IPR
over-reports $N$ ($\Rightarrow$ under-smoothing, the $+0.030$ at $\alpha{=}0.1$). This is the one regime
where the IPR is unreliable. An \textbf{attention-sink counter} (a fixed-score sink key whose post-softmax
weight is $1/(1+N e^{S})$) returns $N=0$ correctly for unseen states and removes the residual; it is a
small, fully hand-set addition (one reserved key). The prediction there is near-uniform regardless, so
the effect on CE is tiny.
\end{invbox}

\begin{takebox}{Design takeaway}
The attention is unchanged --- it only \emph{counts}. The intelligence of this head is two scalar ideas,
both hand-set with no trained weights:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Recover the count, not just the frequency.} The attention's inverse participation ratio
$1/\sum_k A_k^2$ reads off $N$ --- how many times the transition was observed.
\item \textbf{Smooth in the count domain.} $\,(\tilde f N+\varepsilon)/(N+V_r\varepsilon)\,$ is the
Dirichlet posterior-predictive: strong shrinkage when $N$ is small, none when large.
\end{enumerate}
Together they reproduce ctx-count --- the Bayes-optimal context predictor --- and, because the count
magnitudes are kept, the same construction is the optimal context-only predictor for an \emph{arbitrary}
Markov chain, not just equiprobable successors.
\end{takebox}
