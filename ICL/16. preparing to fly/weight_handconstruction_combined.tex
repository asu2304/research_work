\begin{conventionbox}
The combined block stores two parameter tensors per layer and computes, for a row-vector stream
$x\in\R^{n\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$,
\[
s=\frac{\bigl(x\,\Wqk\bigr)x^\top}{\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{out}}}}+M,
\qquad
\mathrm{attn}=\mathrm{softmax}(s)\,\bigl(x\,\Wov\bigr),
\qquad
x'=\bigl[\,x\ ;\ \mathrm{attn}\,\bigr].
\]
So $\Wqk\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $\Wov\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times
d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ are used \emph{as stored} --- $x$ multiplies each from the left. \textbf{Rows
$=$ input dimensions of the query (for $\Wqk$) or of the value (for $\Wov$); columns $=$ input
dimensions of the key (for $\Wqk$) or output slots (for $\Wov$).} No transpose bookkeeping is
needed anywhere in this document.

\medskip
\textbf{Relation to the separate form.} $\Wqk=\Wq^\top\Wk$ and $\Wov=\Wv^\top\Wo^\top$ with the
\texttt{nn.Linear} weights of the separate block. Substituting the separate construction's factors
gives exactly the matrices below; the collapse is bit-exact (verified $0.0$ difference), because
the factors are so sparse that every dot product has at most one non-zero term.

\medskip
\textbf{On the $\sd$ factors.} The score is divided by $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{out}}}=\sqrt{108}$ in
\emph{both} layers --- note that for layer 2 this is $\sqrt{108}$ even though $\Wqk$ is
$216\times216$, because the denominator tracks \texttt{output\_dim}, not the matrix size. Every
$\sd$ written into $\Wqk$ exists only to cancel it. After cancellation the effective coefficients
are the plain integers ($1000$, $50$, $10$, $-10^9$, $-5\times10^4$).
\end{conventionbox}

\section{What changes when the parametrization is combined}

The circuit computes the same function either way. What changes is what a single stored matrix
\emph{means}, and what the parameter space contains.

\begin{interpbox}{Interpretation of $\Wqk$}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Separate.} $\Wq$ and $\Wk$ are two projections into a $108$-dimensional head space,
and the score is an inner product taken \emph{in that space}.
\item \textbf{Combined.} There is no head space. $\Wqk$ is a bilinear form on the residual stream
itself: $s_{t_q,t_k}=x_{t_q}\Wqk\,x_{t_k}^\top$.
\item \textbf{Entrywise.} $[\Wqk]_{i,j}$ is the score contribution when the query has mass on
stream dimension $i$ and the key has mass on stream dimension $j$. Every cell is a statement of
the form ``a query looking like $i$ should (not) attend to a key looking like $j$''.
\item \textbf{One-hot streams make this literal.} Because $x$ is a concatenation of one-hots,
each score is a plain sum of a handful of cells of $\Wqk$ --- no projection to reason about.
\item \textbf{No within-head gauge.} In the separate form $(\Wq,\Wk)\mapsto(M^{-\top}\Wq,M\Wk)$
leaves the function unchanged for any invertible $M$, so $\Wq$ and $\Wk$ are individually
meaningless. That freedom is gone here: $\Wqk$ \emph{is} the observable.
\end{itemize}
\end{interpbox}

\begin{interpbox}{Interpretation of $\Wov$}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Separate.} $\Wv$ reads the stream into head space and $\Wo$ writes it back out ---
two steps.
\item \textbf{Combined.} $\Wov$ is one read--write map. $[\Wov]_{i,j}$ is how much stream
dimension $i$ of the attended position contributes to output slot $j$.
\item \textbf{No gauge either.} $(\Wv,\Wo)\mapsto(\Wv N,N^{-1}\Wo)$ is a symmetry of the separate
form; the combined form has no such freedom.
\item \textbf{Column meaning.} Column $j$ of $\Wov$ is the recipe for what gets written into
concatenated slot $j$, i.e.\ into stream coordinate $d_{\mathrm{in}}+j$ after the concatenation.
\end{itemize}
\end{interpbox}

\begin{warningbox}{Which gauges survive --- and why this experiment removes them}
Two invariances are properties of the softmax and outlive the reparametrization: a
\textbf{temperature} $\Wqk\mapsto c\,\Wqk$ changes sharpness but not the argmax, and a
\textbf{per-query additive shift} $\Wqk\mapsto\Wqk+u\bm{1}^\top$ leaves $\mathrm{softmax}$ rows
unchanged. A third, $\Wov\mapsto\Wov N$ absorbed by whatever reads the output, is normally live
--- but in the folder-17 experiments \textbf{$\Wov$ and the unembedding are frozen to the values
below}, which removes it. Only $\Wqk$ for the two layers is trained, so the comparison against
this construction is as close to gauge-free as the architecture allows.
\end{warningbox}

\begin{notebox}{The rank asymmetry, and why it does not bite here}
In the separate form $\Wq^{(2)},\Wk^{(2)}\in\R^{108\times216}$, so
$\Wqk^{(2)}=\Wq^{\top}\Wk$ can have rank at most $108$ --- half of its $216\times216$ shape. The
combined form stores an unconstrained $216\times216$ matrix. Hence
\emph{separate$\,\to\,$combined collapse is always exact, the reverse is not}: combined is a
strictly larger function class in layer 2.

For \emph{this construction} the distinction is moot: the matrices built below have
$\mathrm{rank}\,\Wqk^{(1)}=101$ and $\mathrm{rank}\,\Wqk^{(2)}=5$, both far under the cap. $W^\star$
is therefore realisable in either parametrization. The asymmetry matters only for what gradient
descent \emph{finds}, not for what the target requires.
\end{notebox}

\begin{warningbox}{Difference from the separate document}
The separate build put the log inside a \emph{residual} FFN, so \texttt{ff2} had to emit
$g(f)=\log(f+\delta)-f$ and the $-f$ cancelled the incoming value. The folder-17 model has
\textbf{no FFN at all}: the log is applied as a function, in place, with no residual around it,
and the pseudo-count is added \emph{before} the log rather than as a logit-space bias afterwards.
\S6 shows the two are algebraically identical.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Input embedding}

There is no learnable embedding. Token $v_t\in\{0,\dots,7\}$ at position $t\in\{0,\dots,99\}$ is
\begin{equation}
x_t=\Bigl[\ \underbrace{e^{(8)}_{v_t}}_{\text{dims }0:8}\ ;\
\underbrace{e^{(100)}_{t}}_{\text{dims }8:108}\ \Bigr]\in\R^{108},
\qquad
M_{t_q,t_k}=-\infty\cdot\bm{1}[t_k>t_q].
\end{equation}

\section{Layer 1 --- the strict previous-signal retriever}

\textbf{Purpose.} Suppress every noise \emph{key}, forbid self-attention, prefer the most recent
admissible position, and copy the token found there into dims $108{:}116$.
Both matrices are $\R^{108\times108}$.

\subsection{$\Wqk^{(1)}\in\R^{108\times108}$}

Rows are indexed by the \textbf{query}'s stream dims, columns by the \textbf{key}'s. Write
$R\in\R^{100\times100}$ for the position sub-block,
\begin{equation}
R_{t_q,t_k}=
\begin{cases}
-10^{9}\sd=-1.0392\times10^{10} & t_q=t_k \quad\text{(no self-attention)}\\[2pt]
-\alpha_{\mathrm{rec}}\sd\,(t_q-t_k)=-207.846\,(t_q-t_k) & t_q\neq t_k \quad\text{(recency)} .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Wqk^{(1)}=
\left[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\bm{0}_{4\times4} & -10^{9}\sd\cdot\bm{1}_{4\times4} & \bm{0}_{4\times100}\\[3pt]
\hline
\bm{0}_{4\times4} & -10^{9}\sd\cdot\bm{1}_{4\times4} & \bm{0}_{4\times100}\\[3pt]
\hline
\bm{0}_{100\times4} & -10^{9}\sd\cdot\bm{1}_{100\times4} & R
\end{array}
\right]}
\begin{matrix}
\rowlab{query signal token, rows $0{:}4$}\\[6pt]
\rowlab{query noise token, rows $4{:}8$}\\[6pt]
\rowlab{query position, rows $8{:}108$}
\end{matrix}
\end{equation}
\[
\underbrace{\phantom{\bm{0}_{4\times4}}}_{\collab{key signal, cols $0{:}4$}}\quad
\underbrace{\phantom{-10^{9}\sd\cdot\bm{1}_{4\times4}}}_{\collab{key noise, cols $4{:}8$}}\quad
\underbrace{\phantom{\bm{0}_{4\times100}}}_{\collab{key position, cols $8{:}108$}}
\]
Non-zero cells: $\bm{10\,432}=432+10\,000$. Rank $101$. Range
$[-1.0392\times10^{10},\ +2.0577\times10^{4}]$.

\begin{interpbox}{Reading $\Wqk^{(1)}$ directly}
The \textbf{entire noise filter is one vertical stripe}: columns $4{:}8$ are $-10^9\sd$ for every
one of the $108$ rows, i.e.\ \emph{whatever} the query looks like, a key whose token is noise gets
$-10^9$. The \textbf{recency rule is the block} $R$, antisymmetric off the diagonal and $-10^9$ on
it. Rows $0{:}8$ carry nothing but the stripe: the query's own token identity plays no role in
layer 1. In the separate form this same content was split as ``$\Wq=\sd I$, and $\Wk$ carries
everything'' --- an artefact of the factorisation, not of the circuit.
\end{interpbox}

\paragraph{Score.} With $x_{t}=[e_{v_t};e_{t}]$ the bilinear form picks out exactly three cells:
\begin{equation}
s_{t_q,t_k}=\frac{x_{t_q}\Wqk^{(1)}x_{t_k}^\top}{\sd}
=\underbrace{-10^{9}\bm{1}[v_k\ge4]}_{\text{col-stripe, twice}}
+\underbrace{-10^{9}\bm{1}[t_k=t_q]}_{R\ \text{diagonal}}
+\underbrace{-20(t_q-t_k)\bm{1}[t_k\neq t_q]}_{R\ \text{off-diagonal}} .
\end{equation}
(The stripe is hit twice --- once from the query's token row, once from its position row --- which
only doubles an already saturating $-10^9$.) Among admissible keys the score is $-20(t_q-t_k)$,
maximised by the largest $t_k$; consecutive candidates differ by $20$ nats, so the softmax puts
weight $\ge 1-e^{-20}$ on the most recent one.

\subsection{$\Wov^{(1)}\in\R^{108\times108}$}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Wov^{(1)}=
\begin{bmatrix}
I_{8} & \bm{0}_{8\times100}\\[2pt]
\bm{0}_{100\times8} & \bm{0}_{100\times100}
\end{bmatrix}}
\begin{matrix}
\rowlab{token dims $0{:}8$}\\[2pt]
\rowlab{position dims $8{:}108$}
\end{matrix}
\end{equation}
Non-zero cells: $\bm{8}$, all $=1$. Rank $8$.
Read by column: output slot $j<8$ receives the attended position's token one-hot coordinate $j$;
slots $8{:}108$ receive nothing, so the $100$ position coordinates of the concatenated output are
identically zero. That is why dims $116{:}216$ of $x^{(1)}$ are $\bm{0}$.

\subsection{What layer 1 writes}
Let $v^*(t)=v_{t^*}$ with $t^*=\max\{t_k<t:\ v_{t_k}<4\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
x^{(1)}_t=\Bigl[\underbrace{e^{(8)}_{v_t}}_{0:8};\ \underbrace{e^{(100)}_t}_{8:108};\
\underbrace{e^{(8)}_{v^*(t)}}_{108:116};\ \underbrace{\bm{0}_{100}}_{116:216}\Bigr]\in\R^{216}.
\end{equation}

\begin{warningbox}{Layer-1 invariant}
For \emph{every} position $t$ --- signal or noise --- dims $108{:}116$ hold $e^{(8)}_{v^*(t)}$,
the most recent preceding signal token. That is the Markov state at time $t$, because noise
tokens do not advance the state.
\end{warningbox}

$\Wov^{(2)}\in\R^{216\times108}$. Its task: attend to every earlier position whose
\emph{predecessor} equals the current state, and average the token one-hots found there.

\item $\bm{B}$'s diagonal, $519.6\,I_4$ at (predecessor)$\times$(key predecessor): the fallback
used when the current token is noise and so has no signal identity of its own.
\item $\bm{K}$'s right half, $-5.196\times10^{6}$ on columns $4{:}8$ for all $8$ rows: the noise
kill --- never attend to a position holding a noise token.
\item $\bm{A}$'s column $4$, $103.9$ for all $8$ rows: the noise trigger, the residue of the
separate form's $\eta$ row. It is a constant added to every score for a given key and is
therefore invisible to the softmax --- it exists only so that the product $\eta\sd\cdot\gamma$
generates $\bm{K}$.
\end{itemize}
In the separate form these four facts were spread across $28$ cells of $\Wq^{(2)}$ and
$\Wk^{(2)}$ and only appeared on multiplication. Here each is a block you can point at.
\end{interpbox}

\paragraph{Score.} For a query at $t$ and key at $t_k$, after dividing by $\sd$,
\begin{equation}
s_{t,t_k}=\underbrace{1000\,\bm{1}[v^*(t_k)=v_t]}_{\bm{A}\text{ diagonal, }v_t<4}
+\underbrace{50\,\bm{1}[v^*(t_k)=v^*(t)]}_{\bm{B}\text{ diagonal}}
+\underbrace{10\,\bm{1}[\text{always}]}_{\bm{A}\text{ col }4}
-\underbrace{5\times10^{4}\,\bm{1}[v_{t_k}\ge4]}_{\bm{K}} .
\end{equation}
If $v_t\ge4$ its one-hot lives in dims $4{:}8$, where $\bm{A}$'s diagonal is zero --- so the
$P_1$ term switches itself off and $P_2$ takes over, with no branching logic anywhere. The noise
term dominates by $50\times$ over $P_1$, so noise positions get weight $\le e^{-5\times10^4}$,
which is exactly $0$ in floating point. Among the survivors,
$A_t=\{t_k<t:\ v_{t_k}<4,\ v^*(t_k)=v_t\}$ scores $1050$ and everything else $0$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{softmax}(s_t)_{t_k}=\frac{\bm{1}[t_k\in A_t]}{|A_t|}\bigl(1+O(e^{-1050})\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{warningbox}{Priority override --- why this is one head, not two}
$P_1/P_2=1000/50=20\times$. When $v_t$ is signal the query matches on the current token; when
$v_t$ is noise the $\bm{A}$ term vanishes and $\bm{B}$ matches on $v^*(t)$. Both branches ask the
same question --- ``which positions had \emph{this state} as their predecessor'' --- because
$v^*(t)=v_t$ whenever $v_t$ is a signal token.
\end{warningbox}

\subsection{$\Wov^{(2)}\in\R^{216\times108}$}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Wov^{(2)}=
\begin{bmatrix}
I_8 & \bm{0}_{8\times100}\\[2pt]
\bm{0}_{208\times8} & \bm{0}_{208\times100}
\end{bmatrix}}
\begin{matrix}
\rowlab{own token dims $0{:}8$}\\[2pt]
\rowlab{all other dims $8{:}216$}
\end{matrix}
\end{equation}
Non-zero cells: $\bm{8}$. Rank $8$.

\begin{notebox}{Why rows $0{:}8$ and not rows $108{:}116$}
$\Wov^{(2)}$ reads the attended position's \emph{original} token, not the layer-1 output sitting
at $108{:}116$. The query side already used the predecessor to \emph{select} which positions to
look at; the value side must then report what token actually occurred there. Averaging those
one-hots is what turns an attention average into a frequency. Choosing rows $108{:}116$ instead
would return the predecessor again --- a constant equal to $e_{v_t}$ --- and the count would carry
no information.
\end{notebox}

\subsection{What layer 2 writes --- why $f$ is a frequency}
\begin{equation}
f_v=\sum_{t_k}\mathrm{softmax}(s_t)_{t_k}\bigl[e^{(8)}_{v_{t_k}}\bigr]_v
=\frac{\#\{t_k\in A_t:\ v_{t_k}=v\}}{|A_t|},
\end{equation}
the empirical successor frequency of the current state. Two exact consequences:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\textstyle\sum_{v=0}^{7}f_v=1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f_v=0\ \ \text{for }v\ge4},
\end{equation}
the first because a softmax sums to one, the second because $A_t$ holds only signal positions.
Measured over $512\times50$ positions: $\max_t|\sum_v f_v-1|=2.4\times10^{-7}$, noise slots zero
to machine precision.

\section{Readout --- direct log with the bias inside}

No FFN. The forward pass applies the log as a function to the eight frequency slots, in place:
\begin{lstlisting}
sl = slice(216, 224)
z = s.clone()
z[..., sl] = torch.log((s[..., sl] + self.readout_bias).clamp_min(1e-30))
return self.fc_out(z)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The pre-log bias vector}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
b=\Bigl[\underbrace{b_{\mathrm{sig}},\dots,b_{\mathrm{sig}}}_{4\ \text{signal slots}},\;
\underbrace{b_{\mathrm{noise}},\dots,b_{\mathrm{noise}}}_{4\ \text{noise slots}}\Bigr]^\top,
\quad b_{\mathrm{sig}}=0.01,\quad
b_{\mathrm{noise}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}})}{4\alpha}\;}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Derivation.} The requirement is $p(v)=\tfrac{1-\alpha}{4}$ for each noise token,
because the generator emits noise with probability $1-\alpha$ spread uniformly over the four noise
tokens, whatever the context. Since $f_v=0$ exactly for $v\ge4$ (\S5.3), the model gives
$p(\mathrm{noise})=b_{\mathrm{noise}}/Z$, so the requirement reads
\begin{equation}
\frac{b_{\mathrm{noise}}}{\,1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}+4b_{\mathrm{noise}}\,}=\frac{1-\alpha}{4}.
\end{equation}
\textbf{Note that $b_{\mathrm{noise}}$ appears on both sides} --- it sits inside $Z$, because
adding noise mass changes the very denominator one divides by. So it must be solved for, not read
off. Writing $S=1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}$ and cross-multiplying,
\begin{align}
4b_{\mathrm{noise}}&=(1-\alpha)\bigl(S+4b_{\mathrm{noise}}\bigr)\\
4b_{\mathrm{noise}}-(1-\alpha)\,4b_{\mathrm{noise}}&=(1-\alpha)S\\
4b_{\mathrm{noise}}\underbrace{\bigl[1-(1-\alpha)\bigr]}_{=\ \alpha}&=(1-\alpha)S
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
b_{\mathrm{noise}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)S}{4\alpha},
\end{align}
which is the boxed formula. \emph{This self-reference is precisely why the naive answer is
wrong}: setting $b_{\mathrm{noise}}=(1-\alpha)/4$ amounts to assuming $Z=1$, i.e.\ forgetting the
mass one has just added. The factor $1/\alpha$ is the correction for it.

\begin{warningbox}{$b_{\mathrm{noise}}$ is a weight, not a probability}
The natural guess $b_{\mathrm{noise}}=(1-\alpha)/4$ is \emph{wrong} here. That form is right only
if the signal slots sum to $\alpha$, which is what happens at random initialisation. The
construction's noise filter drives the noise slots to zero, so the signal slots sum to $1$, and
the constant picks up a $1/\alpha$. At $\alpha=0.8$: $b_{\mathrm{noise}}=0.065$ with
$Z=1.30$, giving $0.065/1.30=0.05$ as required. Using $(1-\alpha)/4$ instead costs $0.11$ nats at
$\alpha=0.4$.
\end{warningbox}

\subsection{Unembedding $W_u$}
A pure selector over all \emph{eight} slots, with no bias:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
W_u^\top=
\begin{bmatrix}
\bm{0}_{216\times8}\\[2pt]
I_8\\[2pt]
\bm{0}_{100\times8}
\end{bmatrix}}
\begin{matrix}
\rowlab{token $+$ position $+$ L1 output, rows $0{:}216$}\\[2pt]
\rowlab{L2 frequency rows $216{:}224$ \ \ \textbf{(active)}}\\[2pt]
\rowlab{unused rows $224{:}324$}
\end{matrix}
\qquad
[W_u^\top]_{216+v,\,v}=1,\ v=0..7 .
\end{equation}
Shape $324\times8$; non-zero cells $\bm{8}$. (The separate document's selector had only $4$,
because the noise logits came from the bias; here they come from $\log b_{\mathrm{noise}}$, so all
eight slots must be read.)

\subsection{Why this gives the exact target distribution}
The logits are $\ell_v=\log(f_v+b_v)$, so the softmax returns
\begin{equation}
p(v)=\frac{e^{\ell_v}}{\sum_u e^{\ell_u}}=\frac{f_v+b_v}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{u}(f_u+b_u)=\underbrace{1}_{\sum f}+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}+4b_{\mathrm{noise}} .
\end{equation}
\textbf{$Z$ is a constant}, independent of the context --- that is what makes a single fixed bias
vector sufficient. Every $f$ has vanished into the $1$, using $\sum_v f_v=1$ from \S5.3, which
holds because the attention output is a weighted average of one-hots with weights summing to one:
\begin{equation}
\sum_v f_v=\sum_v\sum_k a_k\bigl[e_{v_k}\bigr]_v=\sum_k a_k\underbrace{\sum_v\bigl[e_{v_k}\bigr]_v}_{=\,1}
=\sum_k a_k=1 .
\end{equation}
Now substitute $b_{\mathrm{noise}}$, writing $S=1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}$ so that
$4b_{\mathrm{noise}}=(1-\alpha)S/\alpha$, and factor $S$ out:
\begin{equation}
Z=S+\frac{(1-\alpha)S}{\alpha}
 =S\Bigl[1+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\Bigr]
 =S\cdot\frac{\alpha+1-\alpha}{\alpha}
 =\frac{1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}}{\alpha},
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;p(v)=\alpha\,\frac{f_v+b_{\mathrm{sig}}}{1+4b_{\mathrm{sig}}}\ \ (v<4),
\qquad
p(\mathrm{noise})=\frac{b_{\mathrm{noise}}}{Z}=\frac{1-\alpha}{4}\;}
\end{equation}
exactly, with no external renormalisation. The softmax's $\exp$ undoes the $\log$; without the
log it would return $e^{f_v}$ --- exponential rather than linear in the frequency.

$1/25$ each, so $f=[0,\,0.64,\,0,\,0.36,\,0,0,0,0]$ --- note $\sum_v f_v=1$. With
$b_{\mathrm{sig}}=0.01$ and $b_{\mathrm{noise}}=0.065$:

\begin{notebox}{Why $b_{\mathrm{sig}}$ cannot be arbitrarily small}
$f_v=0$ occurs for $26.6\%$ of signal slots even at the construction's own solution --- a
successor the head correctly gives no mass to --- and the true next token lands in one of them on
$0.90\%$ of scored positions at $\alpha=0.4$. So $b_{\mathrm{sig}}$ is a statistical
pseudo-count, not a numerical guard: $b_{\mathrm{sig}}=10^{-38}$ measures test CE $2.3331$
against $1.8430$ at $b_{\mathrm{sig}}=0.01$.
\end{notebox}

\begin{warningbox}{Design takeaway}
Written combined, the whole circuit is \textbf{five sparse blocks and a bias vector}. Every
attention matrix is an identity, a constant stripe, or an antisymmetric ramp --- none performs a
non-trivial linear mixing. The behaviour comes from two properties only:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structured sparsity}: which query dimension is allowed to see which key dimension.
In the combined form this is literally the sparsity pattern of $\Wqk$, readable off the page.
\item \textbf{Scale ratios}: $1000$ vs $50$ makes a hard two-level priority; $-10^9$ and
$-5\times10^{4}$ make effectively infinite suppression.
\end{enumerate}
The separate document needed eight matrices to say this; the combined form needs four, and each
one is the thing gradient descent actually stores.
\end{warningbox}
